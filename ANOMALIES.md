# Anomalies and known weaknesses

This is a plain list of what is wrong with Standpoint, or thin, or open to dispute: what the persona tests, the
fidelity reviews and the content authors found and could not fix. It is kept so that a respondent knows how
far to trust a result, and so that a contributor knows where help is most needed. To dispute anything here, or
add to it, see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

Most entries below are gaps in coverage, not errors: the ontology cannot yet say something that a tradition
would want said. Where a result depends on such a gap, the reading is less sure than it sounds.

## 1. What the journey cannot yet find out

These questions have no problematic, or the positions on them do not exist, so nobody's answer to them is
recorded.

- **Sex work and pornography.** There is no problematic on prostitution, sex work or pornography. Radical
  feminism's signature program (the Nordic model, the anti-pornography ordinances) and the rival
  sex-worker, liberal and queer positions are all missing.
- **The death penalty**, **full indigenous sovereignty** (beyond land back and reconciliation), and a
  **stateless end-state** as a horizon of its own.
- **Strategic doctrines with no position:** Trotsky's transitional program, the Bordigist "the party is its
  program", synthesis anarchism, an Islamist path of development, and "development by invitation".
- **Religious doctrines with no position:** two-kingdoms Lutheranism; the evangelical right's program of
  religious liberty and exemptions; Christian socialism's economics; engaged Buddhism; Buddhist nationalism.
- **Right-wing programs with no position:** neo-reaction's state-as-corporation and exit, clerical fascism,
  the Third Position, paleolibertarian covenant communities, post-Islamist Muslim democracy.
- **Nationalist programs with no position:** Ba'athism's Arab unity, Arab socialism and vanguard party (its only
  defining position is about Rojava); Kemalism's six arrows and étatisme; Peronism's third position and its
  unions; most of Eurasianism, Hindutva and both Zionisms beyond Palestine.
- **Gender programs:** the ethics of care has no position; gender-critical feminism has none on single-sex
  spaces; difference, Islamic and gender-critical feminism define one position each.
- **Technology and ecology:** neo-Luddites and the AI-ethics critics (Gebru, Bender and others) have no
  tradition, so the positions they hold have no defining tradition; the ecomodernist left (Huber, Phillips)
  has no tradition and its objections are filed under Marxism-Leninism; longtermism's governance program
  (compute governance, international AI institutions, its ties to the labs) has no position; neither does
  environmental justice on climate migration, or the Technocracy movement beyond one node.
- **History:** no problematics for Milei, Iraq 2003, Libya 2011, Egypt 2013 or Ayodhya; the descents reach
  these through related positions.
- **Missing traditions** used as stand-ins: post-Keynesianism, the ANC, Nahdlatul Ulama, the Muslim League,
  Kurdish nationalism, neo-Luddism and pacifism are not traditions in the registry.
- **Missing thinkers** (debate sides name them with no thinker entry): Booker T. Washington, Axel Honneth, Silvia
  Federici, Jawaharlal Nehru, Joseph Ratzinger, Voline, Nick Srnicek and Alex Williams, Jason Hickel, Barry
  Commoner, John Zerzan, Bob Black, Hakim Bey.

## 2. Traditions the instrument tells apart poorly

A tradition is only as visible as the positions that define it. These have one or two, or only marginal ones,
so a respondent from them is often read as a nearby tradition.

- **Thin:** individualist anarchism (no Tucker "four monopolies" position apart from mutualism), green and
  primitivist anarchism (no technology or growth positions), feminist anarchism, Juche, structural Marxism,
  Eurocommunism, analytical Marxism, Arab socialism, ordoliberalism (two defining positions), neo-Malthusianism
  (two, making the same claim), effective altruism (global health giving sits only on a common-sense question).
- **Close neighbors with little between them:** minarchist and anarcho-capitalist are separated mainly by
  "the state is a necessary evil"; Marxist-Leninist and Maoist mostly by history and strategy; integralist and Christian democrat only through the religious-politics
  descent. All the neighbor pairs the method requires do separate in the persona tests, but some by a thin margin.
- **Integralism is under-tagged** on natural law, the social function of property, stewardship and the "one
  body" positions.
- **Islamist keystones** (divine law, usury) appear only in the microfinance and family arcs and two descents;
  Survey reaches them when the Situation points there (a devout Muslim on the periphery), seldom otherwise.
- **Ecosocialism and degrowth communism** sit in the Marxist family, so the contrast between ecosocialism and
  degrowth runs across two families instead of within ecology.

## 3. Entries open to dispute

- **Bundled siblings.** Some positions join views their holders keep apart: `alliances.policy.class-against-class`
  merges the Bordigist principle with the Comintern's "third period"; `hist.cold-war.two-superpowers` merges
  Maoist three-worlds theory with the Trotskyist third camp; `body.autonomy.national-need` joins pronatalist and
  antinatalist state policy and now has no defining tradition.
- **One voice for two faiths.** `religion.public.divine-law`, `authority.legitimacy.divine-ordinance` and
  `change.possibility.changed-hearts` define Christian and Muslim traditions but are voiced in one faith's idiom.
- **Near-duplicate:** `property.land.to-the-tiller` and `agrarian.question.land-to-the-tiller`.
- **Tags a reader may reasonably reject:**
  - `feminism.materialist` on the early Soviet social experiment
  - `nationalism.gandhian` on non-alignment (Nehru's line)
  - `nationalism.civic` on the mestizo "mixed nation"
  - black nationalism on "homeland first" migrant loyalty (Garveyite pan-Africanism)
  - pan-Africanism on expropriation without compensation
  - `nationalism.decolonial` on Dirks's caste thesis and on Massad
  - cyberlibertarianism on independent-contractor status and on competing currencies (Hayek's case is Austrian)
  - the Technocracy movement on Brennan's epistocracy
  - Kirk's traditionalists as defining on a clash of civilizations (Huntington fits paleo- and national
    conservatives better)
  - left accelerationism and Chinese socialism as typical on "technology is liberation"
  - Julian Simon under ecomodernism, a tradition that postdates him
- **Contestable lineages:** environmental justice from Christian socialism; degrowth from neo-Malthusianism;
  transhumanism from classical liberalism; the third-worldism entry omits its internal split on the labour
  aristocracy (Amin against Emmanuel and Cope).
- **Contested readings and stock quotes.** `borders.migration.restrict-for-workers` cites Marx's letter on the
  Irish, a reading many Marxists reject. A few famous lines (Luxemburg's "freedom for the one who thinks
  differently", "two, three, many Vietnams", Bakunin's "liberty without socialism") recur across sibling
  positions and blur them. The Leninist and Maoist argument on `culture.strategy.power-first` cites
  postliberal agreement. Minarchism's key texts include Barnett's *The Structure of Liberty*, which leans
  polycentric.
- **Sources.** Bernard Yack is cited for the civic-nationalist position he argues against, with no supporting
  classic; Rodinson's essay is dated 1967 in one place and 1973 in another (the French and English first
  editions); Carson's *Studies in Mutualist Political Economy* and Dahrendorf's *Class and Class Conflict*
  carry two different years; the title of the 2022 post that founded e/acc is unconfirmed.
- **Accelerationism.** `accelerationism.question.collapse-it` is reachable only by rejecting other options:
  no option endorses it, by design.

## 4. Journey

- Two arcs are set in Nairobi, two in India and two in France.
- The nation-and-land descent opens on the 2020 Wet'suwet'en pipeline dispute, close to the pipeline arc;
  Greece in July 2015 appears in two descents, from opposite sides of the table.
- Several scenes are invented composites (a dam in Laos, a port in Santos) and are labelled as such; scenario
  wage and price figures are the authors' estimates of order of magnitude.

## 5. Engine and reading

The persona suite (42 answer policies run through Survey) is the engine's test bed. As of 1.0:

- **Survey is a sample.** About 46 decisions cannot touch 1,250 positions. Each run takes the trunk, two or
  three arcs chosen by the Situation and by the traditions nearest the answers so far, a descent, pressure
  scenes, and three targeted probes (one that could complete a tension, one that splits the respondent's
  nearest rival traditions, one next to a keystone). Across the personas, 11 different arcs come first and 66
  of 74 expected keystones are recovered.
- **Positions mostly reached by probes are checked rarely.** Common-sense positions such as `cs.decline` sit
  mainly on probes; the declinist persona never meets one, so its keystone goes unchecked.
- **The tension probe leans toward intervention on the left.** Almost every left respondent half-meets the rule
  "holds a class program at home and backs intervention abroad", so many are asked an intervention probe.
- **The smallest neighbor margin is ordoliberal against liberal technocrat** (0.3 on the own-tradition-first
  check). Both separate, but barely.
- **Arc relevance tables live in code.** Which arc suits which Situation is written in
  `packages/core/src/relevance.ts`, not in the arc files, so a new arc gets no Situation weighting until the
  table is edited.
- **Results depend on which third arc a run gets.** Several findings (the self-reliant shopkeeper, the Marxist
  who reasons by villains, the radical's reading of the empire abroad) appear only if a particular arc is
  chosen. The selection weights were tuned until all checks passed, not to an optimum.
- **The mobilization arc's escalation has no liberal-internationalist option**, so a liberal interventionist
  sent there is read as anti-imperialist on that node.

- **The reading fires on what was asked.** A tension rule or a keystone check can only fire if Survey reached the
  nodes that carry its positions. Whatever a run did not reach, the reading says nothing about; silence is not
  absence.

## 6. The frame

The reading follows the materialist frame stated in [METHOD.md](METHOD.md): it asks who gains, who pays and
through what mechanism. Traditions that reject that question (liberals who see politics as reasonable
disagreement among individuals, religious traditions that read history providentially, conservatives who
distrust any theory of society as a whole) will find their own views described in their own terms and then
read in another's. That is a declared standpoint, not a hidden one, and the result offers "other readings" in
each tradition's voice, but the main reading is not neutral and does not claim to be. Disputes that the frame
itself is wrong are recorded here as they come in.
